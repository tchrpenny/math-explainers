Try–Discuss–Connect

The routine behind most i-Ready Classroom Mathematics lessons in grades 2–5. Tap each stage, then try the small activity inside it.

Around the routine, a lesson session usually opens with a short Start and finishes with a Close.

Sample problem used below (grade 3 multiplication)
A baker puts 6 muffins on each tray. She fills 4 trays. How many muffins does she bake in all?
Students make sense of a new problem and try it on their own before anyone shows them a method.

Students

  • Hear the problem more than once and talk about what it means.
  • Work alone with drawings, tools, or equations.
  • Choose the tools that make sense to them, such as counters, grid paper, number lines, or base-ten blocks.

You

  • Start with a language routine like Three Reads or Notice and Wonder. It opens the problem without handing over a strategy.
  • Walk the room, listen, and ask students to tell you about their work.
  • Quietly note which strategies appear. You will use that list in Discuss It.

Partners explain and defend their thinking. Then you choose the work worth showing the whole class.

Students

  • Explain their strategy to a partner and say why they chose it.
  • Listen, ask questions, and respectfully challenge each other's reasoning.
  • Share with the class when their work is selected.

You

  • Offer partner prompts so talk goes beyond "I got 24."
  • Select and sequence two or three strategies, often from most concrete to most abstract.
  • Ask students to reword vague statements so the math language gets precise.
  • Treat mistakes as something the class learns from.

The class compares strategies and links them to a more abstract idea.

Students

  • Compare how different models show the same quantities.
  • Answer guided questions that look back at the problem, look ahead to a new idea, and reflect.
  • Keep choosing tools that support their reasoning.

You

  • Lead a whole-class compare-and-connect conversation.
  • If nobody showed a key model, such as an array or a number line, bring it in and have students study how it works.
  • Listen for the big idea, not only the right answer.

Connect It questions for this problem

Look back

Explain how you found the number of muffins. What did your picture or equation show about the trays?

Look ahead

Is there a faster way than counting every muffin? How could the number of trays and the muffins per tray help?

Reflect

Which model helped you most today? How are 4 × 6 and 6 × 4 alike, and how do they differ in the story?

The 20% that gives 80% of the payoff

  • Protect the quiet solo time in Try It. Hold back hints.
  • Choose strategies on purpose in Discuss It, and order them so each builds on the last.
  • Finish Connect It by naming what the strategies have in common.

Common slips

  • Rescuing students during Try It, which skips the productive struggle.
  • Sharing work as show and tell with no comparing.
  • Using Connect It only to check the answer.
